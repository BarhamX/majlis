# Majlis Business Requirements Document

## 1. Business Objective

Build a culturally authentic daily mobile game that turns Arab cultural knowledge into a recurring habit, then monetize through premium content, institutional licensing, cultural partnerships, and sponsored cultural campaigns.

## 2. Business Problem

Arab culture content is abundant but scattered. Users consume heritage content passively through social media, but there is limited product infrastructure for daily cultural challenges, family competition, moderated discussion, and structured learning around Arab identity.

## 3. Business Opportunity

Majlis can occupy the gap between language learning apps, social apps, quiz games, and cultural education. Its strongest wedge is the daily ritual: one official Majlis per day that users discuss, solve, and share.

## 4. Customer Segments

### B2C

- Arab youth.
- Families.
- Arab diaspora.
- Culture-curious users.

### B2B / Institutional

- Schools.
- Universities.
- Cultural organizations.
- Museums and heritage centers.
- Festivals and public cultural programs.

## 5. Value Proposition

Majlis helps Arabs rediscover and share cultural knowledge through a short daily game that feels like a modern digital majlis: competitive, warm, social, and respectful.

## 6. Monetization Model

### Freemium

Free users get the daily Majlis, streaks, basic discussion, and limited archive access.

### Premium Subscription

Premium users get archive access, advanced regional packs, audio storytelling, private family Majlis, deeper explanations, and streak protection.

### Cultural Courses

Short paid packs around topics such as proverbs, poetry, hospitality, Arab history, dialects, food culture, or regional traditions.

### School and University Licenses

Institutional dashboards, private classrooms, weekly reports, teacher prompts, and curated challenge packs.

### Sponsorships

Cultural organizations can sponsor themed weeks, heritage campaigns, museum events, or national cultural days.

## 7. MVP Business Requirements

- Free daily challenge must be valuable enough to attract organic sharing.
- Shareable cards must create low-cost acquisition.
- Content admin workflow must allow daily publishing without developer involvement.
- Basic analytics must measure retention and challenge completion.
- Community safety must exist before public growth campaigns.

## 8. Revenue Assumptions

Initial revenue should not be the MVP's primary success measure. First prove:

1. Users return daily.
2. Users share challenges.
3. Families/friends compete.
4. Content can be produced reliably.
5. Discussion remains respectful.

After this, premium and institutional models become credible.

## 10. Business KPIs

- Daily active users.
- Weekly active users.
- D7 retention.
- Challenge completion rate.
- Share conversion rate.
- Invites sent per active user.
- Premium conversion rate.
- Cost per activated user.
- Content production cost per daily Majlis.
- Moderation cost per 1,000 comments.

## 11. Launch Strategy

Phase 1: Closed alpha with friends/family and cultural reviewers.

Phase 2: Qatar/Gulf-focused beta with daily challenges and share cards.

Phase 3: Regional packs and family leagues.

Phase 4: University/cultural organization pilots.

Phase 5: Pan-Arab expansion with region-specific editorial partners.

## 12. Business Decision Gates

### Gate 1: Alpha Fit

- 100-300 test users.
- More than 50% first challenge completion.
- Users understand the value within 30 seconds.

### Gate 2: Retention Fit

- D7 retention shows daily loop promise.
- Users complete multiple challenges in first week.
- Share cards produce installs or waitlist signups.

### Gate 3: Community Fit

- Discussion responses are meaningful.
- Moderation load is manageable.
- Family/friend groups show higher retention than solo users.

### Gate 4: Monetization Fit

- Users request archive, private groups, audio, or regional packs.
- Institutions express interest in classroom/cultural use.
